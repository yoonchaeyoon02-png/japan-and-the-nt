01 — THE OPPORTUNITY

Why the NT?

The Northern Territory is well positioned to strengthen Australia's relationship with Japan and contribute to a more connected and prosperous Indo-Pacific. Darwin's strategic northernmost location makes it an important "gateway" between Asia and Australia.

The Northern Territory is closer to five foreign capital cities than to Canberra. This location creates opportunities to strengthen ties with Japan and other surrounding Asian countries.

Along with this, Australia and Japan already share a strong relationship built on alliances in trade, investment, energy and regional security. Japan is an important trading partner, especially for the NT, as Japan heavily contributes to the export of resources such as liquefied natural gas (LNG).

Our starting point

The NT already has important connections with Japan. The next step is to strengthen these relationships by connecting people and creating opportunities for future generations.

02 — THE VISION

How?

I would champion a Northern Territory and Japan collaboration partnership, focusing on connecting people. This initiative would create more opportunities for young Territorians and Japanese communities to connect, learn and work, building lasting relationships.

STRATEGY 01

People: Youth, Education and Sport

The first strategy would be to establish a well-known NT–Japan Youth Exchange Program. This association would work towards connecting schools, students and sporting organisations in both countries.

🌏

Student Exchange

Students could participate in overseas exchanges, experiencing new cultures and meeting new people. This would provide opportunities to engage with other cultures, gain communication skills and have fun.

However, overseas exchange is not always possible, so an alternative would be to create online classroom projects. Students from both regions could communicate and create presentations and projects together on shared issues, such as climate change.

日本語

Language and Culture

Students in the NT could also become more engaged in Japanese language programs and cultural experiences. Japan is a holiday destination for many families, so learning Japanese would not only strengthen ties but also be a handy skill to have.

It would also build communication skills and a deeper understanding of Japan.

🏐

Sport and Teamwork

Sport would also be another key factor for the partnership. Sport is one of the many things that brings people together. Japanese sporting teams could travel to the NT for training camps or competitions, while NT athletes could go to Japan.

Australia specialises in sports like swimming and athletics, while Japan is known for its volleyball and baseball. Athletes could learn from each other's training and improve their skills together.

This could bring people together through teamwork, discipline and respect. Darwin could become a destination for Japanese sporting teams, potentially attracting visitors and supporting local sporting organisations.

STRATEGY 02

Prosperity: Skills, Jobs and Innovation

This second strategy would focus on strengthening economic connections between Japanese companies and the Northern Territory. I would encourage partnerships between Japanese businesses, universities and local organisations in areas like renewable energy, technology and critical minerals.

These partnerships could attract investment, support local business and boost the Territory's economy. For example, collaboration in renewable energy and technology could help develop new industries while creating job opportunities for future generations.

☀️

Innovation and Industry

Partnerships in renewable energy, technology and critical minerals could help Japanese and NT organisations share knowledge, develop new ideas and create opportunities for economic growth.

🎓

Career Opportunities

I would advocate for internships, apprenticeships, scholarships and mentoring programs that connect Japanese and NT students. Students could visit countries they are interested in for work purposes and potentially build a career in a new country.

This would allow young people to gain valuable experiences, learn from professionals and prepare for their careers.

💡

Darwin–Japan Innovation Forum

An annual Darwin–Japan Innovation Forum could bring together researchers, entrepreneurs, investors and industry leaders to learn new business strategies and explore opportunities.

By connecting Japanese communities with local talent, the Territory could encourage innovation and build stronger economic partnerships that benefit both countries.

STRATEGY 03

Peace and Resilience: Building a Stronger Indo-Pacific

The third strategy would promote cooperation in disaster preparedness and environmental conservation. Australia and Japan both experience natural disasters, although the risks and intensity differ between the two countries.

🛟

Disaster Preparedness

Schools, communities and emergency management organisations could share their knowledge, develop educational campaigns and participate in joint training to strengthen disaster resilience.

🌿

Environmental Conservation

Environmental collaboration could encourage both countries to share ideas about protecting ecosystems and preserving the natural environments that future generations will depend on.

🤝

Cultural Knowledge

I would also support cultural and environmental exchanges involving First Nations communities in the NT, where appropriate. These exchanges could focus on traditional land management, ancestral knowledge and ecological preservation, guided by the communities involved.

Cooperation beyond trade

Together, these strategies would create a partnership built not only on trade, but also on genuine connections between people. By connecting people, developing skills and encouraging collaboration, the NT could play an important role in strengthening Australia's relations with Japan.

OUR SIGNATURE INITIATIVE

The Japanese Passport

One of the major strategies I would like to implement is a Japanese Passport. This idea originated from the passport used in the nationwide program The Rite Journey. I would present it as a youth exchange and international cultural exchange program rather than a competition.

THE ULTIMATE OPPORTUNITY

A Fully Funded Trip to Japan

Students who successfully complete their passport by the end of Year 12 would qualify for a fully funded trip to Japan. This would give them the opportunity to experience the country, practise their Japanese, meet local students and strengthen the connections they have built.

This is a proposed reward that would depend on securing government funding, grants or sponsorship from partner organisations.

03 — THE IMPACT

Why does it matter?

Strengthening the relationship between the NT and Japan is about more than trade. It is about creating opportunities for people to learn from one another, build trust and work together towards a better future.

Peace

Cultural understanding, student exchanges and lasting friendships can help build trust and mutual respect between communities.

Prosperity

Economic partnerships, innovation and career pathways could create opportunities for young people and support businesses in both countries.

Security and Resilience

Cooperation in disaster preparedness and environmental conservation could help communities share knowledge and prepare for common challenges.

A partnership that starts with people.

By connecting people, developing skills and encouraging collaboration, the Northern Territory can help strengthen Australia's relationship with Japan and contribute to a peaceful, prosperous and secure Indo-Pacific.
